3. Close reading: the MAIN causes · 32 minutes

Read each short passage. Identify the key idea, then answer the critical-thinking question. Use the vocabulary definitions when needed.

M — Militarism

Before 1914, major European powers invested heavily in armies, navies, and military technology. Germany and Britain competed to build powerful fleets. Military leaders prepared detailed plans to mobilize troops quickly. These preparations were intended to protect countries and discourage attacks. But when a crisis arrived, the pressure to act quickly made diplomacy more difficult.

Vocabulary: militarism; mobilize; diplomacy.

Did stronger militaries make Europe safer, or did they make war more likely? Explain the tension between these two possibilities.

Building a stronger military could protect a country by…

However, it might also increase the danger of war because…

A — Alliances

European countries formed partnerships to increase security. Before the war, Germany and Austria-Hungary were closely aligned, while France and Russia were allies; Britain also had important understandings with France and Russia. These relationships were not all identical treaties requiring automatic military action. In the crisis of 1914, leaders feared that failing to support partners would weaken their security and influence. A dispute involving Austria-Hungary and Serbia spread as additional powers made decisions about mobilization and war.

Vocabulary: alliance; ally; crisis.

How could alliances intended to prevent attacks help turn a regional conflict into a much larger war?

An alliance might discourage attacks because…

Yet alliances could expand a conflict when…

I — Imperialism

European empires competed for influence, resources, trade, and territory in Africa, Asia, and other regions. Britain and France controlled large overseas empires, while Germany sought greater international influence. Competition created mistrust and diplomatic crises, including disputes over Morocco. Imperial rivalry did not directly cause every decision made in July 1914, but it helped produce an atmosphere of competition among great powers.

Vocabulary: imperialism; colony; rivalry.

How might competition for colonies far from Europe increase distrust among countries in Europe?

Imperial competition could increase tension because…

This may have mattered even when a dispute happened in Europe because…

N — Nationalism

Nationalism encouraged people to identify strongly with their nation and support its independence, power, or prestige. In multinational empires such as Austria-Hungary, some ethnic and national groups wanted greater independence. Serbian nationalism and South Slav nationalist movements challenged Austrian authority in the Balkans. Nationalism could unite people, but competing national goals also created conflict.

Vocabulary: nationalism; Balkans; self-determination.

When can pride in a nation become a source of conflict? Use the Balkans as an example.

4. The spark: Sarajevo · 10 minutes

On June 28, 1914, Gavrilo Princip, a Bosnian Serb nationalist, assassinated Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary in Sarajevo. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia and issued an ultimatum. After diplomatic efforts failed, Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia on July 28. Russian mobilization and German, French, and British decisions contributed to the widening war in early August.

Why is the assassination best described as a trigger rather than the only cause of World War I?

The war might not have spread as widely without…

5. Rank the MAIN causes · 15 minutes

Rank the four causes from 1 (most significant) to 4 (least significant). Every cause must appear once. This is a historical argument, not a single-answer quiz.

6. Claim and defend · 15 minutes

Why did you rank your #1 cause above your #2 cause? Use evidence from at least two readings and explain why your #1 was more influential.

Although … also contributed to the war, … was more significant because…

Partner challenge: What is the strongest objection to your ranking? How would you respond using evidence?
